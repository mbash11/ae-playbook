Pre-Call Research for SaaS AEs Using AI — The 5-Minute Brief

Two Perplexity searches. One Claude prompt. Five minutes. A sharper brief than 30 minutes of tab-hopping across LinkedIn, Crunchbase, and Google — every single time.

Pre-call research is the most underestimated time drain in SaaS sales. It does not feel like admin because it looks like preparation — but 30 minutes of unfocused tab-hopping across LinkedIn, the company website, Crunchbase, and Google News often produces a collection of surface-level facts rather than strategic insight.

AI pre-call research for SaaS AEs changes this entirely. The system below takes five minutes, produces a six-point structured brief that is more actionable than anything you would find in 30 minutes of manual research, and works for any prospect at any deal stage.

Before — 30 minutes

LinkedIn profile scan. Company website about page. Google News search. Crunchbase funding data. Their Twitter or X account. Five tabs open. Surface-level facts. No synthesis. No clear angle.

After — 5 minutes

Two Perplexity searches. One Claude prompt. A six-point structured brief with company context, pain points, likely objections, a sharp opening question, and a personalisation hook.

Step 1 — The two Perplexity searches

Perplexity synthesises web results into a single clean output instead of returning ten links to read individually. Run these two searches before every call:

Perplexity search 1 — Company context
"What are the most recent news, announcements, or developments for [company name] in the last three months?"
Perplexity search 2 — Role context
"What are the main business challenges or priorities for a [prospect's job title] at a [company size and industry] company in 2026?"

Copy both outputs. You now have current company context and role-specific pain points — the two inputs that make the brief prompt produce genuinely useful output.

Step 2 — The pre-call brief prompt

Paste both Perplexity outputs into this prompt along with the prospect details and your product context:

Pre-call brief prompt
"You are helping a SaaS AE prepare for a sales call. Research: [paste both Perplexity results]. Prospect: [name, title, company]. My product helps [company type] with [value prop]. Call stage: [discovery / demo / follow-up / commercial]. Generate a brief with six sections: 1. What is going on at this company right now. 2. What this person probably cares most about. 3. Most likely reason they would be interested in my product. 4. Most likely objection and how to handle it. 5. One sharp opening question. 6. One personalisation hook from the research. Brief and specific. No filler."

What a good pre-call brief looks like

A well-structured pre-call brief from this system covers six specific points. Here is what each section should contain:

  • Company context. One or two sentences on what is happening at the company right now — recent funding, a new product launch, a leadership change, a public statement about priorities. Something you can reference naturally in the first two minutes of the call.
  • What the prospect cares about. Based on their role, company size, and industry — what is the most likely pressure they are operating under. Not a generic job description, but a specific inferred priority.
  • Why they would be interested. The specific connection between their context and your product's value proposition. This is what you lead with after the opening question.
  • Most likely objection. The one most probable pushback based on their role, their company size, or what you know about the deal stage — with a brief framing of how to handle it if it comes up.
  • Sharp opening question. One question that demonstrates you have done your homework and invites them to talk about something they actually care about. Not "tell me about your business."
  • Personalisation hook. One specific detail from the research you can reference naturally — a recent company announcement, a LinkedIn post they made, a product launch. The kind of detail that signals genuine preparation.

Using this for multi-stakeholder enterprise deals

For enterprise deals with multiple contacts in the room, run the brief prompt once for the account overall and once for each individual you know will be on the call. For a meeting with four stakeholders that takes under 15 minutes and gives you a brief tailored to each person's role and likely agenda.

Save each brief in a folder called "Account Briefs" with the account name and date. Over time this becomes your institutional knowledge on every account — pull it when revisiting a stalled deal, prepping for a renewal, or briefing a sales engineer before a technical demo.

The question that separates good pre-call research from great pre-call research is not "what do I know about this company" — it is "what does this specific person care about and how does my product connect to that." The brief prompt is structured to answer the second question, not the first.
