How to use these AI prompts for SaaS sales

Every prompt below works with Claude, ChatGPT, or any other large language model. Before using any prompt for the first time, read it through and fill in the bracketed fields with your specific context. The more specific you are with the input, the more usable the output will be straight away.

The goal is not to send the AI output verbatim. The goal is to get to 80% of a finished email or document in 60 seconds rather than writing from scratch, then spend two minutes editing the remaining 20% to match your voice.

Post-call follow-up prompts

These prompts take a Fathom call summary or your own notes as input and produce a personalised follow-up email. Use the discovery call prompt after every first meeting, and keep the others available for specific situations.

Discovery call follow-up
"You are an experienced SaaS AE. I just finished a discovery call. Here is the summary: [paste call summary]. My product helps [company type] with [value prop]. Write a follow-up email under 200 words. Conversational tone. Summarises key discussion points, confirms next steps, ends with a clear CTA."
Objection-heavy call follow-up
"SaaS AE. Tough call with several objections raised. Call summary: [paste summary]. Biggest objection: [objection]. Write a follow-up under 180 words. Acknowledge the objection honestly, offer one supporting point without being defensive, propose a specific next step."
Stale deal re-engagement
"SaaS AE. Deal gone quiet. Account context: [paste brief or last interaction summary]. Last meaningful contact: [timeframe]. Write a re-engagement email under 150 words. Does not sound desperate. Acknowledges time passed naturally. Offers value or reason to reconnect. Proposes a specific next step."

Pre-call research prompts

Use these after running two Perplexity searches on the account and the contact's role. Paste the Perplexity output into the prompt below to generate a structured brief.

Pre-call brief
"You are helping a SaaS AE prepare for a sales call. Research: [paste Perplexity results]. Prospect: [name, title, company]. My product helps [company type] with [value prop]. Stage: [discovery/demo/follow-up]. Generate a brief with six sections: 1. What is going on at this company right now. 2. What this person probably cares most about. 3. Most likely reason they would be interested. 4. Most likely objection. 5. One sharp opening question. 6. One personalisation hook. Brief and specific. No filler."

Pipeline review prompts

These three prompts replace two hours of Friday pipeline admin with a 20-minute structured review session. Run them in order at the same time each week.

Pipeline health check
"You are helping a SaaS AE review their pipeline. Open deals: [paste one-sentence summary of each deal with stage and last activity date]. For each deal: one-sentence assessment, green/amber/red health rating, one suggested action. At the end list the three most at-risk deals with one sentence explaining why. Be direct, not optimistic."
Manager report
"SaaS AE preparing weekly pipeline report. Pipeline: [paste updated pipeline summary]. Write a report covering: total pipeline value and active deal count, forecast breakdown by close likelihood, top three deals with status and next step, key risks this week, what I am doing to advance pipeline. Direct and confident. No padding."

What makes these prompts work better than generic AI prompts

  • They are built around real sales contexts. Each one specifies the role, the situation, and the output format — rather than leaving the AI to guess what a sales follow-up should look like.
  • They specify length and tone. Telling the AI to write under 200 words in a conversational tone produces a much more usable output than asking it to write a follow-up email.
  • They use real inputs. Pasting an actual call summary produces output calibrated to what was said in the conversation, not a generic template.
  • They specify what not to do. Phrases like "not defensive" and "no padding" remove the most common failure modes in AI-generated sales content.
