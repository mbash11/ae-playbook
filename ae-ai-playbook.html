Chapter 01
The Admin Audit — Find Your 10 Hours
You cannot fix something you haven't measured. This chapter gives you a 20-minute process to find out precisely where your time is going and a clear first target to eliminate.

The five admin buckets

Every SaaS AE's non-selling time falls into one of five categories. Most people bleed across all of them, but almost everyone has one or two that genuinely dominate.

  • CRM logging. Updating deal stages, logging call outcomes, adding contact notes, keeping activity records current. This happens after every single interaction.
  • Follow-up writing. Personalised post-call emails, recap summaries, proposal follow-ups, check-in messages to stalled deals. This one is deceptive because it feels like selling.
  • Pre-call preparation. Researching the company, reviewing LinkedIn, checking recent news. Done properly this takes 20 to 30 minutes per call.
  • Pipeline reporting. Weekly updates for your manager, forecast submissions, deal commentary, QBR prep. Usually two hours on a Friday pulling data that already existed somewhere.
  • Internal comms and handoffs. Briefing sales engineers, updating SDRs, looping in customer success. The invisible bucket nobody counts as admin but absolutely is.

The 20-minute time audit

For one working week, every time you switch tasks, add a tally mark to the relevant bucket. At the end of the week, count and estimate time spent.

Your automation priority

Ask three questions about each bucket. The one with three yeses is where you start.

  • Does it happen at least three times a week? Frequency makes automation worthwhile.
  • Does it follow a repeatable pattern? AI excels at consistent inputs producing consistent outputs.
  • Does it require real-time human judgment? If no, it's your target.

Chapter 03 · System 1
The Follow-Up That Writes Itself
Post-call admin currently costs the average AE 25 minutes per call. With this system running, it costs five. Five copy-paste prompts. Fathom doing the heavy lifting.
%
The post-call tax

AEs spend an average of 20 to 30 minutes on post-call admin for every hour-long call. At four calls a day that's up to two hours of post-call admin every single day before you've moved a single deal forward.

The five follow-up prompts

Prompt 1 — After a discovery call
"You are an experienced SaaS AE. I just finished a discovery call. Here is the summary: [paste Fathom summary]. My product helps [company type] with [value prop]. Write a follow-up email under 200 words. Conversational tone. Thanks them briefly, summarises key discussion points, confirms next steps, ends with a clear CTA."
Prompt 2 — After a demo
"SaaS AE here. Just delivered a demo. Summary: [paste Fathom summary]. Prospect most interested in [feature]. Main concern: [objection]. Write a follow-up under 200 words that acknowledges their interest, addresses the concern briefly, and proposes a next step."
Prompt 3 — After an objection-heavy call
"SaaS AE. Tough call with several objections. Summary: [paste Fathom summary]. Biggest objection: [objection]. Write a follow-up under 180 words. Acknowledge honestly, offer one supporting point, propose a specific next step. Not defensive. Not overselling."
Prompt 4 — After a commercial conversation
"SaaS AE. Commercial conversation, prospect close to decision. Summary: [paste Fathom summary]. We discussed pricing and [what was agreed]. Write a follow-up under 200 words confirming what was discussed, next steps with owners, tone of confidence not pressure."
Prompt 5 — When nothing was decided
"SaaS AE. Call with no clear next step agreed. Summary: [paste Fathom summary]. Write a follow-up under 150 words. Honest about where we are, proposes a specific next step with timeframe, keeps door open without being desperate."

The CRM update prompt

CRM notes prompt
"Here is a summary of a sales call I just had: [paste Fathom summary]. Write structured CRM notes: Current deal stage. Key discussion points (3 bullets max). Main objection raised. Agreed next steps with owner and timeframe. Deal health 1-10 with one sentence reasoning."

What this adds up to

Before
4 calls × 25 min post-call = 100 min daily, roughly 8 hours per week on tasks that generate zero pipeline movement.
With this system
4 calls × 5 min post-call = 20 min daily. 6 hours per week returned to selling time from this system alone.

Chapter 04 · System 2
The 5-Minute Pre-Call Brief
Two Perplexity searches. One Claude prompt. Five minutes. A better brief than 30 minutes of unfocused tab-hopping — every single time.

Step 1: the Perplexity searches

Perplexity search 1
"What are the most recent news, announcements, or developments for [company name] in the last three months?"
Perplexity search 2
"What are the main business challenges or priorities for a [prospect's job title] at a [company size and industry] company in 2026?"

Step 2: the pre-call brief prompt

Pre-call brief prompt
"You are helping a SaaS AE prepare for a sales call. Research: [paste Perplexity results]. Prospect: [name, title, company]. My product helps [company type] with [value prop]. Stage: [discovery/demo/follow-up]. Generate a brief with these six sections: 1. What's going on at this company right now. 2. What this person probably cares most about. 3. Most likely reason they'd be interested. 4. Most likely objection. 5. One sharp opening question. 6. One personalisation hook. Brief and specific. No filler."

The multi-stakeholder brief

Run the brief prompt once for the account overall and once per contact. For an enterprise deal with four stakeholders you'll have five briefs in under 15 minutes. Stack them in one document and you walk in knowing every person in the room.

Build your brief library

Save every brief in a folder called "Account Briefs" alongside updates from Fathom summaries. Over time this becomes your institutional knowledge on every account. Pull it when revisiting a stalled deal, prepping for a renewal, or briefing a sales engineer.

Takeaway

Two Perplexity searches and one Claude prompt in five minutes. Better than 30 minutes of tab-hopping. Save the prompt and use it before your next call tomorrow.

Chapter 05 · System 3
The Pipeline Review That Takes 20 Minutes, Not Two Hours
Three copy-paste prompts. One 20-minute Friday ritual. Clean pipeline, manager report that writes itself, stale deals re-engaged every week.
Prompt 1 — Pipeline health check
"You are helping a SaaS AE review their pipeline. Open deals: [paste one or two sentence summary of each deal with stage and last activity date]. For each deal: one-sentence assessment, green/amber/red health rating, one suggested action. At the end, list the three most at-risk deals with one sentence each explaining why. Be direct, not optimistic."
Prompt 2 — Manager report
"SaaS AE preparing weekly pipeline report. Pipeline: [paste updated pipeline summary]. Write a report: Total pipeline value and active deals. Forecast breakdown (likely close, stretch, early stage). Top three deals with one paragraph each covering status, last interaction, next step, close probability. Key risks. What I'm doing this week to advance pipeline. Direct and confident. No padding."
Prompt 3 — Stale deal re-engagement
"SaaS AE. Deal gone quiet. Account context: [paste brief or last interaction summary]. Last meaningful contact: [timeframe]. Write a re-engagement email under 150 words. Doesn't sound desperate. Acknowledges time passed naturally. Offers value or reason to reconnect. Proposes specific next step. Warm and direct."

The 20-minute Friday ritual

  • Minutes 1 to 5: Write a one or two sentence summary of each open deal from your CRM.
  • Minutes 6 to 10: Run Prompt 1. Read output. Note red deals.
  • Minutes 11 to 14: Update the two or three most out-of-date deal stages in your CRM.
  • Minutes 15 to 18: Run Prompt 2. Copy the manager report output.
  • Minutes 18 to 20: Paste report wherever your manager expects it. Done.
